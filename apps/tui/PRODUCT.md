# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who read long-form books in a terminal, including Chinese and English
readers who want keyboard-first navigation and optional local read-aloud without
leaving the reading flow. Their primary task is to choose a book, keep their
place, and read or listen with as little interface management as possible.

## Product Purpose

readio is a terminal ebook reader that borrows the interaction grammar of a
coding agent. It turns real book structure, progress, search, bookmarks, and
local text-to-speech into one coherent keyboard-driven reading experience.
Success means the interface stays out of the prose while every consequential
choice remains explicit, inspectable, and reversible.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, precise, literary. The interface should feel capable and calm rather
than playful, ornamental, or mechanical.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a media player, a streaming-service catalogue, a dense settings
dashboard, or a transcript filled with numbered pseudo-controls. Avoid musical
iconography, promotional model cards, hidden automatic engine switching, and
download actions whose size or destination is disclosed only after they start.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the book, not the controls, at the centre of attention.
2. Make one decision appear in one place, with its scope stated plainly.
3. Say what the machine will do before it does it, especially for downloads.
4. Prefer honest system state over optimistic labels.
5. Make every persistent choice easy to inspect, replace, or return to default.
6. Keep acquiring a model separate from configuring how and where it is used.
7. Keep Read-aloud distinct from Auto: TTS is a hard token clock. Slow speech
   makes tokens wait; unavailable or failed speech stops without falling back.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support complete keyboard operation, clear focus and selected states, readable
contrast in dark terminals, CJK-aware layout, and bilingual Chinese/English
copy. Do not rely on colour alone for installation, active, warning, or error
states. Keep motion minimal and ensure progress remains understandable without
animation.
